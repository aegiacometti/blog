Hi adrian,  
After publishing the Manual Work Is a Bug blog post, I got this feedback from Michele Chubirka explaining why automating changes in your network also increases network security:  
• I can restrict access to devices except through programmatic methods. Direct access to a device or host should be the exception, not the rule. Have “break glass” methods to access console in an emergency.  
• I can also write security and compliance playbooks to validate that automation code is compliant with standards and baselines. This is much easier for governance teams to review and validate.  
• I can keep all deployment code in a single repository and manage it just like all my other code, making revision control and integrity management much easier.  
• By using automation, just like a CI/CD pipeline, I can use the same deployment methodology: blue/green deployments, rollbacks, etc….  
But the most important thing is that with automation, I can create immutable devices in alignment with the rest of my DevOps “cattle.” No more pet or snowflake hardware. It’s not just about eliminating manual work, but creating repeatable, modular processes. The goal is to think like a programmer and less like a pieceworker in a sweat shop.  
Want to get there? Start with network automation webinars to get your bearings and join the network automation online course to build your first network automation solution.

Cuales son los problemas... tiempo... personas... rotacion... skilles en la empresa... skilles personales

Even if you are a manager or an engineer, face it, you need it, sooner or later you will have to go into this topic, and better sooner than later right?

Because this is not something you can go to buy it and in 2 weeks you are done, maybe you can if you want a checked bullet for marketing purposes, but that will fell down as soon as it's born.

Every situation is different as every company has different needs and contexts, so you have to encode just that, your needs. There are several typical ones that will arise soon and it will help you to get on board, but some others are specific to your context.

See it like training a new employee, but this time the employee will never leave the company, hence that encoded knowledge is there, it will evolve and will be always available to answer your questions (like a chatBot).

As an engineer, is the kind of knowledge that will open new doors. On the other hand, not having it, there are not too many new doors to open, just the same ones you always have in front of you. If you are truly an engineer, you like challenges and learn new things, well, this is the new thing.

In my personal experience, I was tired of managing devices individually, constantly having to learn new topologies, re learn the big ones I already forgot, or that specific command you use once in a while, login to several devices to see the logging or some specific configuration. That takes a lot of time. When I saw the opportunity of managing devices as a whole, it cracked my mind.

What if I can have the answer to this question in less than a minute without even having to login to every device:

- show me the logging of all the devices that have "core" in their name
- show me the BGP prefixes of all the MPLS devices
- show me clock time configuration

You tell me how much time in troubleshooting you are saving here eeeeevery time you have to check on some incident or trouble ticket.

And then you go and make some other knowledge available to the Helpdesk:

- change a user port VLAN?
- Or when somebody calls you at 3 AM to reset a VPN

They can do it alone with a simple playbook!

Isn't it a waste of our time to keep doing those tasks every time? Not because we are gods, just because $$$, and is boooooring!

What about an action that takes time and shouldn't be necessary for a person to do it, like a "WAN link down" event:

- Trigger alarm
- Open a ticket
- Send email to ISP
- Send chat to supports
- Even open a chat room with the #issue and invite the support team to work on it

Well, this can be done almost instantly with automation. Good one right?

But wait! Automation is not that new either, it's old as programming!

Yes, basically, every program is a way to process information and automate some kind of task or decision, like collecting information and showing this web page.

Aha yes, but that doesn't change the fact that we are being slow in delivery time to end users, and end users are in the need of faster answers. So, this affirmation just give more impulse to what I'm saying here.

Start coding your infrastructure. It doesn't need to be huge, just start, and at some point benefits will arrive. In the form of fast response, better show-off of what you are doing for the company, reducing troubleshooting times, don't having a hole if an employee leaves, and in fact maybe encoraging an employee to stay because is learning something new. After all if you can't get the employee to stay which is completely normal (change is always good and part of our lives), at least part of his knowledge is staying in the company in the form of code, from which you can still have some benefit.

User adoption can be tricky like in any other tool, **always keep in mind working on their needs**, work on use cases, check trouble tickets, talk to supports, talk to Helpdesk or even other IT departments. Sooner or later it will kick-off. But if you don't do this... it will fail as soon as you buy the new fancy tool that does every David copperfield trick but nobody wants to use because is complicated. So go opennsource. Is cheaper and if it fails is free. This is not just having a beautiful inventory and alarm system. This goes beyond and integrates everything as a whole ecosystem.

Later, since you are doing this in code, you can easily do analytics and engagement. Who is using this, was the result satisfactory, hey John Doe try this new thing, etc.

I'm not talking here about automayed stacks of appliaction deployment onprem or in the cloud. That's another topic. But how can you starting doing that if you are not doing all this other things that I'm mentioning here which are directly related to the networking skill. So first go with this, and later start talking the same automation lenguaje with the other IT teams.

There are a lot of tools out there as many consultants as me or even great tutorials that can help you to get started with this.
